## Background: From Attention to Reasoning

The evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) from static transduction engines to autonomous agents is rooted in the fundamental transition from sequential processing to parallelized attention mechanisms. Prior to the introduction of the Transformer architecture [Vaswani et al., 2017], sequence modeling was dominated by Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). These architectures suffered from an inherent sequential bottleneck, where the hidden state at time $t$ depended on the state at $t-1$, strictly limiting parallelization during training. [Vaswani et al., 2017] addressed this by proposing a model architecture relying entirely on multi-head self-attention, which allows the model to draw global dependencies between input and output positions without regard to their distance in the sequence.

The core innovation of the Transformer lies in the Scaled Dot-Product Attention mechanism, defined as $\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})V$. By projecting queries, keys, and values into multiple representation subspaces, the Multi-Head Attention mechanism enables the model to jointly attend to information from disparate positions. This architectural shift yielded significant performance gains, achieving 28.4 BLEU on the WMT 2014 English-to-German task and 41.8 BLEU on English-to-French, while simultaneously reducing training costs through massive parallelization. These foundational improvements in computational efficiency facilitated the scaling of models to the parameter regimes necessary for the emergence of complex cognitive capabilities.

As model scale increased, researchers observed that standard few-shot prompting—which relies on providing simple input-output pairs—was insufficient for tasks requiring multi-step logical deduction, such as arithmetic, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic manipulation. The breakthrough in addressing this limitation was the discovery of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [Wei et al., 2022]. Unlike standard prompting, CoT encourages the model to generate intermediate natural language reasoning traces before arriving at a final answer. This method effectively decomposes complex problems into a sequence of manageable sub-steps, allowing the model to allocate more computational resources to harder reasoning tasks.

Empirical evidence indicates that CoT is an emergent ability, typically becoming effective only in models exceeding approximately 100B parameters. For instance, [Wei et al., 2022] demonstrated that while a PaLM 540B model using standard prompting achieved 17.9% accuracy on the GSM8K benchmark, the application of CoT prompting increased this performance to 56.9%. Similarly, on the StrategyQA dataset, CoT improved accuracy from 68.6% to 77.8%. These results suggest that the reasoning process is not merely a byproduct of knowledge retrieval but a structural capability elicited by the sequential decomposition of thoughts.

Despite these advancements, CoT-based reasoning remains susceptible to logical gaps and hallucinations, as the models are constrained by their internal weights and lack grounding in external reality. The "black box" nature of these reasoning traces often leads to "one-step missing errors" or incorrect arithmetic operations, as shown in the failure modes analyzed by [Wei et al., 2022]. Furthermore, while CoT allows for improved performance, it does not inherently provide the model with up-to-date information or the precision required for complex symbolic calculations. This limitation necessitates a transition from purely internal reasoning to systems that can ground their outputs in external tools, such as search engines and calculators. The synthesis of architectural parallelization [Vaswani et al., 2017] and the emergent reasoning elicited by CoT [Wei et al., 2022] provides the necessary infrastructure for the next phase of development: the integration of autonomous tool-use and persistent memory, which transforms static language models into proactive, goal-oriented agents. The following sections will analyze how this trajectory continues through the interleaving of reasoning and action.
