## Related Work

The evolution of large language models (LLMs) has transitioned from static sequence transduction, as established by the Transformer architecture [Vaswani et al., 2017], toward dynamic, agentic systems. This shift is characterized by three primary research trajectories: the enhancement of internal reasoning, the integration of external tools, and the management of long-term memory.

### Reasoning and Grounding
The foundational capacity for multi-step reasoning in LLMs was significantly advanced by the introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [Wei et al., 2022]. By eliciting intermediate reasoning traces, CoT enables models to decompose complex tasks, achieving 56.9% accuracy on the GSM8K benchmark. However, CoT remains inherently static and prone to hallucination, as it lacks a grounding mechanism to verify intermediate logical steps against factual reality. Addressing this, [Yao et al., 2023] proposed the ReAct framework, which interleaves reasoning traces with external tool interaction. By grounding model outputs in environmental observations, ReAct achieved a 71% success rate in the ALFWorld decision-making environment. Despite this, ReAct relies heavily on prompt engineering and often suffers from repetitive looping, highlighting a limitation in the model's ability to autonomously manage the transition between internal reflection and external action.

### Self-Supervised Tool-Use
Recent efforts have shifted toward automating the acquisition of tool-use capabilities to reduce reliance on human-annotated demonstrations. [Schick et al., 2023] introduced Toolformer, a framework that utilizes a self-supervised approach to teach models how to autonomously select and invoke APIs. By filtering potential API calls based on perplexity reduction—specifically, keeping calls that lower the cross-entropy loss by a threshold $\tau_f$—the model learns to use tools such as calculators and search engines. While Toolformer demonstrates superior performance on benchmarks like ASDiv (40.4% accuracy vs. 14.0% for GPT-3), it is constrained by a lack of multi-step tool chaining. The model cannot naturally ingest the output of one tool as the input for another, a limitation that necessitates a more integrated, recursive architecture for complex problem-solving.

### Memory-Augmented Agentic Architectures
Parallel to tool-use research, the development of generative agents has focused on maintaining long-term social and contextual coherence. [Park et al., 2023] demonstrated that by implementing a persistent memory stream—comprising observations, reflections, and plans—agents can simulate believable human behavior in sandbox environments. Their architecture utilizes a retrieval function based on recency, importance, and relevance to synthesize high-level insights, achieving a TrueSkill rating of 29.89 in controlled evaluations. However, this level of coherence comes at a prohibitive computational cost. The frequency of reflection cycles and the management of expanding memory buffers create a bottleneck that limits the scalability of such agentic systems. 

### Synthesis and Research Gap
The current literature presents a fragmented landscape. While [Wei et al., 2022] provides the logic for reasoning and [Yao et al., 2023] provides the grounding for acting, the lack of a unified mechanism for chained tool interaction remains a critical gap [Schick et al., 2023]. Furthermore, the high computational overhead associated with the memory and reflection cycles identified by [Park et al., 2023] prevents real-time deployment in complex, multi-hop reasoning tasks. 

Our proposed Unified Agentic Transformer (UAT) addresses these gaps by moving beyond prompt-based strategies. Unlike existing approaches that treat memory, reasoning, and tool-use as decoupled components, UAT integrates these into a unified latent process. By finetuning the model to treat memory retrieval and tool invocation as a cohesive sequence, UAT enables the model to store intermediate tool outputs back into the memory stream. This allows for multi-hop reasoning over tool results, effectively resolving the chaining limitation identified in [Schick et al., 2023]. Furthermore, by optimizing the reflection mechanism through a learned latent representation, we aim to mitigate the computational overhead inherent in the memory-augmented architectures described by [Park et al., 2023]. This synthesis of foundational Transformer scaling [Vaswani et al., 2017] with agentic capabilities represents a novel approach to building robust, self-supervised, and memory-efficient reasoning agents.
