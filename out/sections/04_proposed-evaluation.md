## Proposed Evaluation

To assess the efficacy of Cognitive Orchestration, we propose a comprehensive evaluation protocol designed to measure the performance of our framework against existing state-of-the-art agentic architectures. The evaluation will be conducted across three distinct domains: arithmetic reasoning, interactive decision-making, and grounded fact verification. By utilizing a range of model sizes, we aim to evaluate the scalability of our recursive memory-reflection loop and the utility of the memory tuple $M = (t, c, a, o, r)$, where $t$ denotes the timestamp, $c$ the context, $a$ the action, $o$ the observation, and $r$ the reflection.

We formalize our experimental objectives through three primary hypotheses. First, we hypothesize that recursive tool chaining (H1) improves performance on multi-step arithmetic tasks compared to the Toolformer baseline [Schick et al., 2023]. This hypothesis posits that by enabling the agent to decompose complex problems and chain tool calls, we can address the single-step limitation identified in previous self-supervised tool-use models. Second, we hypothesize that reflection-augmented memory (H2) improves long-term task coherence in simulated environments, surpassing the performance of Generative Agents without reflection [Park et al., 2023]. Finally, we hypothesize that our retrieval score $S(q, m) = w_1 \cdot \text{sim}(q, c) + w_2 \cdot \exp(-\lambda \cdot (t_{\text{now}} - t_m))$ (H3) provides a mechanism to ground reasoning in contextually and temporally relevant information, which we plan to evaluate against standard ReAct [Yao et al., 2023].

We will employ a robust ablation strategy to isolate the contributions of specific components within the Cognitive Orchestration framework. Specifically, we plan to evaluate the framework by removing the reflection component ($r$) to determine its impact on long-term coherence, as well as disabling the recursive tool-chaining mechanism to assess its necessity for multi-step reasoning tasks. These ablations will be compared against the full architecture to ensure that the observed performance is attributable to the intended mechanisms.

To mitigate threats to validity, such as prompt sensitivity and in-context learning variance, we will standardize all prompts across baselines and our proposed framework. We will perform head-to-head comparisons using models of identical size and architecture where possible, ensuring that the evaluation focuses on the structural advantages of Cognitive Orchestration.

The benchmarks selected represent a test of agentic capabilities. GSM8K and ASDiv will test the agent's ability to perform multi-step arithmetic reasoning, addressing the limitations of single-step tool usage [Schick et al., 2023]. ALFWorld will serve as the primary testbed for interactive decision-making, where the agent must navigate simulated environments [Yao et al., 2023]. Finally, fact verification tasks will allow us to quantify the effectiveness of our retrieval score $S(q, m)$ in grounding the agent's reasoning trace $r$. 

We anticipate that the recursive nature of our framework may introduce increased inference latency compared to standard prompting methods. We plan to monitor the relationship between chain depth and inference cost, providing an analysis of the efficiency of Cognitive Orchestration as a function of the complexity of the reasoning task. By systematically evaluating these components, we aim to demonstrate that Cognitive Orchestration provides a scalable approach to autonomous agentic reasoning, bridging the current gap between immediate reasoning accuracy and long-term behavioral coherence. We plan to evaluate the model's ability to effectively utilize the memory update process through the proposed prompting-based objective, which focuses on the coherence of the reflection trace $r$ given the observation $o$. Future iterations may explore fine-tuning to further optimize these parameters, but our initial evaluation will prioritize the zero-shot and few-shot capabilities inherent in the proposed architecture.
